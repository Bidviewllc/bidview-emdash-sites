Hearing Evaluations

Understanding your hearing health starts with a thorough evaluation. Our specialists use advanced testing to detect hearing loss and develop personalized solutions that fit your lifestyle.

Which hearing test is the best?

The best hearing test depends on your needs. A comprehensive hearing evaluation performed by an audiologist is the most accurate way to assess your hearing. Common tests include:

  • Pure-tone audiometry – Determines the softest sounds you can hear at different frequencies.
  • Speech audiometry – Evaluates how well you understand spoken words.
  • Tympanometry – Checks for middle ear problems like fluid buildup or eardrum issues.
  • Otoacoustic emissions (OAE) testing – Measures inner ear function, often used for newborns and children.
  • Auditory brainstem response (ABR) testing – Assesses how the brain processes sound, used for more complex hearing concerns.

A professional hearing test is the most reliable way to diagnose hearing loss and determine the best treatment options.

Can I test my own hearing?

You can do a basic self-check for hearing loss by noting symptoms such as:

  • Difficulty following conversations
  • Increasing the volume on the TV or phone
  • Frequently asking others to repeat themselves

There are also hearing test apps and online screenings that can provide a general idea of your hearing ability. However, these tests are not a substitute for a professional hearing evaluation. If you suspect hearing loss, it’s best to schedule a comprehensive test with a hearing aid specialist.

Are online hearing tests any good?

Online hearing tests can be a useful starting point, but they have limitations. They typically measure how well you hear certain tones through headphones or speakers but do not assess:

  • Speech comprehension
  • Middle ear function
  • The cause of hearing loss

Factors like background noise, device quality, and test conditions can also affect accuracy. While online tests may indicate potential hearing problems, they should not replace a full evaluation by a professional. If you have concerns about your hearing, a diagnostic test from a hearing aid specialist or audiologist is the best option.

Tinnitus Evaluations and Treatment

Experiencing ringing or buzzing in your ears? Our tinnitus evaluations identify the cause, and our treatment options—such as sound therapy and management strategies—help provide lasting relief.

Should I go to an audiologist for tinnitus?

Yes, an audiologist is one of the best professionals to assess and manage tinnitus. If you’re experiencing persistent ringing, buzzing, or humming in your ears, an audiologist can:

  • Perform a tinnitus evaluation to identify possible causes.
  • Check for underlying hearing loss, which is often linked to tinnitus.
  • Recommend treatment options, such as sound therapy, hearing aids with tinnitus masking features, and relaxation techniques.
  • Provide coping strategies to help reduce the impact of tinnitus on your daily life.

If tinnitus is disrupting your sleep, concentration, or overall well-being, scheduling an appointment with an audiologist can help you find relief.

Is tinnitus a brain problem?

While tinnitus originates in the ears, research suggests it is a neurological issue involving the brain’s auditory processing system. When hearing loss or ear damage reduces sound input, the brain may compensate by creating phantom sounds, which we perceive as tinnitus.

Because tinnitus is linked to brain activity, stress, and auditory function, treatments often focus on managing perception rather than eliminating the sound entirely. Sound therapy, cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), and hearing aids can all help the brain adjust and minimize tinnitus awareness.

What happens if tinnitus is not treated?

Tinnitus itself is not life-threatening, but if left unmanaged, it can lead to:

  • Increased stress, anxiety, or irritability
  • Difficulty concentrating or sleeping
  • Worsening hearing issues, especially if linked to untreated hearing loss
  • Emotional distress, including frustration, depression, or social withdrawal

The longer tinnitus persists, the more it can affect your mental and emotional well-being. Seeking professional help can improve your quality of life and make tinnitus easier to manage.

Diagnostic Services

We offer comprehensive diagnostic testing to assess your hearing and uncover any underlying conditions. Our team ensures accurate results and guides you toward the best course of action.

What are diagnostic hearing tests, and why are they important?

Diagnostic hearing tests are specialized evaluations that assess your hearing ability and detect any underlying conditions affecting your ears. These tests help determine the type, severity, and cause of hearing loss, allowing audiologists to recommend the best treatment options, whether it’s hearing aids, medical intervention, or other hearing solutions.

What types of diagnostic hearing tests are performed?

Audiologists use a variety of tests to assess hearing health, including:

  • Pure-tone audiometry – Measures your ability to hear different tones at various pitches and volumes.
  • Speech audiometry – Evaluates how well you hear and understand spoken words.
  • Tympanometry – Checks middle ear function to detect issues like fluid buildup or eardrum problems.
  • Otoacoustic emissions (OAE) testing – Assesses inner ear function, often used for newborns and young children.
  • Auditory brainstem response (ABR) testing – Evaluates how the brain processes sound, often used for neurological assessments.

When should I get a diagnostic hearing test?

You should consider a diagnostic hearing test if you experience:

  • Difficulty hearing conversations, especially in noisy environments
  • Frequent ringing or buzzing in your ears (tinnitus)
  • Muffled sounds or a sensation of fullness in the ears
  • Sudden or gradual hearing loss
  • Dizziness or balance issues

If you notice any of these symptoms, scheduling a hearing evaluation with a hearing aid specialist or audiologist can help identify potential hearing problems and guide you toward the best treatment options.

Ear Wax Removal

Built-up ear wax can block sound and cause discomfort. Our professional removal services safely clear your ears, improving your hearing and overall ear health.

What is the best way to remove ear wax?

The safest way to remove ear wax is through professional ear cleaning by a hearing aid specialist or audiologist. However, if you have mild buildup, you can try these safe at-home methods:

Over-the-counter ear drops – These help soften wax so it can naturally exit the ear.
Warm water rinse – Using a bulb syringe with lukewarm water can gently flush out softened wax.
Ear irrigation (by a professional) – A healthcare provider can safely remove stubborn wax using specialized tools.
Microsuction (by an audiologist) – A gentle, precise method that removes wax without water.

Avoid using cotton swabs, bobby pins, or other objects, as they can push wax deeper into the ear and cause blockages or damage.

Do ear candles work?

No, ear candles are not a safe or effective method for removing ear wax. Research shows they do not create enough suction to pull out wax, and they pose risks such as:

  • Burns to the ear and face
  • Wax residue from the candle entering the ear canal
  • Increased ear blockage if wax is pushed deeper

For safe ear wax removal, stick to medically approved methods or visit a professional for assistance.

What does dark brown ear wax mean?

Ear wax color can vary depending on age, environment, and ear health. Dark brown or black ear wax is usually older wax that has been exposed to air and oxidized over time. It is more common in individuals with higher ear wax production or those who have gone longer without cleaning their ears.

In some cases, very dark wax may indicate excess buildup that needs professional removal. However, if you experience pain, a sudden change in wax color, or discharge, consult an audiologist or doctor to rule out infection or other ear conditions.

Hearing Aid Fittings

A properly fitted hearing aid enhances comfort and clarity. We customize your device to match your hearing needs, ensuring a seamless and natural listening experience.

How long does it take to get fitted for a hearing aid?

The hearing aid fitting process typically takes about one to two hours. During this appointment, the audiologist will adjust the settings to match your hearing needs, ensure a proper fit, and teach you how to use and care for your device. If custom earmolds are required, it may take one to two weeks for them to be made before your final fitting. Follow-up visits may also be needed to fine-tune the device and ensure optimal performance.

How does an audiologist fit a hearing aid?

A hearing aid specialist or audiologist fits a hearing aid by:

  1. Programming the device – Using the results from your hearing test, the audiologist or hearing aid specialist adjusts the settings to match your level of hearing loss.
  2. Ensuring proper fit – The hearing aid is placed in your ear and adjusted for comfort and security.
  3. Real-ear measurement (REM) – A small microphone is used to check how sound is amplified inside your ear canal, ensuring the hearing aid provides the right level of amplification.
  4. Testing and fine-tuning – The hearing aid specialist or audiologist will play sounds and speech to ensure clarity and comfort.
  5. Providing instructions – You’ll learn how to insert, remove, clean, and maintain your hearing aids.

Follow-up appointments are often scheduled to make further adjustments based on your experience with the device.

Can you get a hearing aid without a test?

Some over-the-counter (OTC) hearing aids are available without a professional hearing test, but they are designed for mild to moderate hearing loss and do not provide the same level of customization as prescription hearing aids.

Getting a comprehensive hearing evaluation from an audiologist ensures that your hearing aids are programmed specifically for your needs. A professional test can also determine whether your hearing loss is caused by a medical condition that requires treatment rather than just amplification. If you’re unsure about your hearing loss, consulting with an audiologist is the best way to get the right solution.

Hearing Aid Repair

If your hearing aid isn’t working properly, we offer quick and reliable repairs to restore its performance. From minor fixes to more complex issues, we’ve got you covered.

What are common problems that require hearing aid repair?

Hearing aids can experience various issues over time. Common problems include:

No sound or weak volume – This could be due to a clogged wax filter, a dead battery, or internal damage.
Distorted or muffled sound – May result from moisture buildup, a faulty microphone, or a damaged receiver.
Whistling or feedback noise – Often caused by improper fit, earwax buildup, or malfunctioning components.
Physical damage – Cracks, broken battery doors, or loose parts may need professional repair.

How long does a hearing aid repair take?

Repair time depends on the issue. Minor fixes, like cleaning or replacing a wax filter, can often be done in the clinic within a few minutes. More complex repairs, such as internal component replacement, may require the hearing aid to be sent to the manufacturer, which can take several days to a few weeks.

Can you get a hearing aid without a test?

Some minor issues can be resolved at home, including:

  • Replacing the battery
  • Cleaning the microphone and wax filter
  • Checking for moisture and using a hearing aid dehumidifier
  • Ensuring the volume and settings are properly adjusted

However, if the hearing aid is still not working properly, do not attempt to open or repair internal components yourself. This could cause further damage. Instead, contact your audiologist or hearing aid specialist for professional repair.
