Why Would I Need a Hearing Test?

Regular hearing tests can help detect hearing loss early. If you struggle to hear conversations, turn up the TV volume, or notice ringing in your ears, it’s time to get tested.

Hearing loss can develop slowly, making it hard to notice at first. Common signs include:

  • Struggling to hear in noisy places
  • Asking people to repeat themselves
  • Turning up the volume on the TV or phone
  • Ringing or buzzing in the ears

What Are the Types of Hearing Tests?

  1. Pure-Tone Audiometry – Determines the softest sounds you can hear at different frequencies.
  2. Speech Audiometry – Measures how well you understand words at various volume levels.
  3. Tympanometry – Checks for middle ear problems, like fluid buildup or eardrum issues.

Three Main Types of Hearing Loss

  1. Sensorineural Hearing Loss – Caused by damage to the inner ear or hearing nerve. Often permanent and treated with hearing aids or cochlear implants.
  2. Conductive Hearing Loss – Occurs when sound can’t travel through the outer or middle ear. Often treatable with medication or surgery.
  3. Mixed Hearing Loss – A combination of sensorineural and conductive hearing loss, requiring specialized treatment.

Hearing Test: What to Expect

During your test, you’ll wear headphones and listen to different tones. A hearing aid specialist will also check your ear health and ask about your hearing history. The test is painless and takes about 30 to 45 minutes.

Hearing Test Results

Once the test is complete, the hearing aid specialist will explain your results using an audiogram (a graph of your hearing ability). If hearing loss is found, we will discuss treatment options, such as hearing aids or medical referrals.

Hearing Test FAQs

Can I test my own hearing?

You can take an online hearing test, but it won’t be as accurate as a professional hearing test.

Will I need to do anything to prepare for a hearing test?

No special preparation is needed, but try to avoid loud noise before your test.

Are there any risks to hearing tests?

No, hearing tests are safe and painless.

How reliable are online hearing tests?

Online tests can give you a rough idea of your hearing, but they are not as precise as a professional hearing exam. Rose Hearing Healthcare Centers provides comprehensive hearing tests in Hilton Head Island & Bluffton, SC.

Can you do a hearing test on your phone?

Some apps provide basic hearing checks, but they can’t replace a professional hearing evaluation.

How often should I take a hearing test?

Adults with normal hearing should have a hearing test every 3 to 5 years. If you have hearing loss, wear hearing aids, or work in a noisy environment, you should get a hearing test once a year.
