Sudden Hearing Loss? Here's What You Need to Know

If you woke up and suddenly noticed hearing loss in one or both ears and you know it's not from a concert, gunfire, or other loud noise exposure, it's worth taking seriously. Sudden hearing loss can come from a few different places: a simple mechanical blockage like earwax or fluid behind the eardrum, or something happening deeper in the inner ear itself.

A second category, sudden sensorineural hearing loss (SSNHL), is the one that requires urgency. It can be triggered by a viral or autoimmune inflammatory response, or by a vascular event that interrupts blood flow to the inner ear, similar in concept to a stroke. Because the inner ear can't be examined just by looking in the ear canal, only a hearing test can tell you which type you're dealing with.

Timing matters enormously here. The sooner SSNHL is identified and treated (ideally within the first 72 hours to two weeks), the better the odds of recovering meaningful hearing, often with a short course of oral or injected steroids. The longer treatment is delayed, the more those odds decline, and after about a month, the chances of full recovery drop significantly. Unfortunately, this is exactly what we see too often: people wait days or weeks assuming it will resolve on its own, or get bounced between providers before the story is fully heard and the case gets flagged as a true sudden hearing loss.

What to do: If you wake up with sudden, unexplained hearing loss in one or both ears, don't wait it out. Call us right away. We prioritize getting patients with sudden hearing loss in for testing as quickly as possible, because in this case, time is hearing.

Audiology Services in College Station, TX

Our diagnostic services go beyond a standard hearing test. Using advanced technology — real-ear measurements, tympanometry, acoustic reflex testing, and more — we deliver the detailed information needed for accurate diagnosis and personalized treatment at every age.

+Hearing Evaluations

A comprehensive hearing evaluation is the first step toward better hearing. We test how well you hear across a range of frequencies and identify the type and degree of any hearing loss (including sensorineural, conductive, and mixed hearing loss). We'll walk you through exactly what the results mean for your daily life. We provide evaluations for patients of all ages, from newborns to adults.

+Pediatric Hearing Evaluation

Children don't always have the words to tell you when something doesn't sound right. We provide comprehensive pediatric hearing evaluations, including newborn follow-up OAE screenings, ear-specific testing, tympanometry, and advanced ABR/ASSR diagnostic assessments when needed. Visual Reinforcement Audiometry (VRA) is available for infants as young as 4–6 months and Conditioned Play Audiometry for young children. We create a calm, child-friendly environment, using age-appropriate techniques and advanced technology to obtain reliable results for families and referring physicians.

+Veterans C&P Hearing Evaluations

We conduct comprehensive Compensation and Pension (C&P) hearing and tinnitus examinations for veterans authorized through the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs. Every evaluation is thorough, professionally documented, and completed with the respect every veteran deserves.

+Musicians' Hearing Evaluations

Musicians rely on their hearing more than almost any other professional, yet they're also among the most vulnerable to noise-induced hearing loss and tinnitus. Standard hearing tests only measure up to 8kHz, missing early damage that often begins at higher frequencies.

At Liberty Hearing Center, we offer extended high-frequency testing up to 16kHz, allowing us to detect subtle changes in hearing before they affect your ability to perform, mix, or enjoy music. Regular monitoring helps musicians catch early warning signs, make informed decisions about hearing protection, and preserve the fine pitch and tonal discrimination their careers depend on. We also offer custom-fit musician earplugs and consultations tailored to your instrument, practice habits, and performance environment.

Learn more →
+Customized Ear Protection Solutions

Not all hearing protection is created equal, and off-the-shelf options often mean sacrificing comfort, safety, or sound quality. At Liberty Hearing Center, we design custom-fit hearing protection tailored to your lifestyle.

For musicians, we offer custom earplugs with interchangeable 9dB, 15dB, 20dB, and 25dB filters, reducing volume evenly across frequencies so the music stays clear while your hearing stays protected.

We also fit custom in-ear monitors for singers and performers, giving them full control over the mix — how loud they hear their own vocals, other instruments, and the crowd — for a safer, more precise on-stage experience.

Swimmers and those with ear tubes can benefit from custom swim plugs that help prevent water intrusion and swimmer's ear. For hunters and shooters, we offer custom firearm earplugs that enhance speech and ambient sound during quiet moments, then instantly engage to protect against the sudden, damaging noise of a gunshot.

We provide comprehensive evaluations for patients of all ages, including high-frequency hearing assessments for musicians, ototoxicity monitoring, advanced tympanometry and acoustic reflex testing, newborn hearing evaluations with OAE, ABR, and ASSR technology, pediatric testing (including Visual Reinforcement Audiometry), sudden hearing loss evaluations, hearing aid performance analysis and verification, real-ear measurements, cochlear implant services, and custom hearing protection for musicians, hunters, and others exposed to hazardous noise. Our advanced technology delivers the detailed information needed for accurate diagnosis, personalized treatment, and exceptional hearing healthcare.

Frequently Asked Questions

+I woke up with sudden hearing loss — should I be worried?

Yes, if there's no obvious cause like loud noise exposure. Sudden hearing loss can signal an inner ear issue that responds well to treatment — but only if caught early, ideally within the first 72 hours to two weeks. Call us right away so we can test your hearing and get you connected to treatment fast.

+What audiology services do you provide?

We provide comprehensive hearing care for patients of all ages, including adult and pediatric hearing evaluations, newborn hearing screenings, veteran C&P examinations, tinnitus evaluations, hearing aid fittings, cochlear implant programming, ear wax removal, and custom hearing protection.

+Do you serve veterans?

Yes. We conduct VA-authorized Compensation and Pension (C&P) examinations, evaluating hearing loss and tinnitus for veterans in the College Station and Brazos Valley area. We also extend special discount pricing on hearing technology to active-duty military and veterans as part of our service appreciation program.

+What hearing aid brands do you carry?

We carry hearing aids from Phonak, Oticon, ReSound, Widex, Starkey, and Signia, and we match each patient with devices that fit their specific hearing loss, lifestyle, and budget, and every fitting includes real-ear verification to confirm the devices are calibrated correctly for each of your ears.

+What pediatric hearing services do you offer?

We provide a full range of pediatric hearing services, including OAE-based newborn follow-up screenings, Visual Reinforcement Audiometry (VRA) for infants as young as 4–6 months, ABR and ASSR testing for children who need objective measures, and diagnostic evaluations for school-age children who do not pass school hearing screenings or have concerns about speech and language development.

+Do you accept insurance?

We accept Medicare, Blue Cross Blue Shield, United Healthcare, and authorized referrals through the VA Community Care Network. We'll verify your benefits and explain any out-of-pocket costs before your appointment so there are no surprises. Contact us to learn more.

+How do I know if I need a hearing aid?

A hearing aid may be recommended when a confirmed hearing loss is affecting your daily life. Common signs include difficulty understanding conversations in quiet or in noisy environments, frequently asking others to repeat themselves, or turning the TV up louder than others prefer.

Hearing loss can also take an emotional toll. If conversations with family and friends become frustrating — for you or the people you're talking with — or if you find yourself avoiding social situations because communicating feels stressful or embarrassing, it may be time to explore hearing aids. Persistent ringing in the ears (tinnitus), especially when accompanied by hearing loss, is another reason to schedule a comprehensive hearing evaluation to determine the most appropriate treatment options.
