Hearing Evaluations in College Station, TX

A hearing evaluation is not a pass/fail screening. It is a clinical appointment designed to give you a complete picture of how your auditory system is functioning — and what that means for your daily communication, relationships, and quality of life.

  • A hearing evaluation is a comprehensive diagnostic appointment that identifies the type, degree, and impact of hearing loss through detailed testing and an in-depth review of your hearing history.
  • Most people wait seven to ten years after noticing hearing difficulty before seeking care. Early evaluation gives you accurate information, more options, and a better long-term outcome.
  • We provide thorough, unhurried adult and pediatric hearing evaluations for patients throughout the Brazos Valley who are ready for real answers about their hearing.

It starts with your story

Before a single test begins, Dr. Chris Duhon, AuD, or Dr. Carly Hall, AuD, takes time to understand your hearing history.

  • Where have you worked over the years?
  • Have you spent time around loud equipment, firearms, or live music?
  • Are there specific situations — restaurants, phone calls, family gatherings — where following conversation has become harder than it used to be?

This case history is not a formality. The patterns you describe shape the diagnostic picture and help distinguish between different causes of hearing difficulty. A person who spent twenty years in a manufacturing facility and a person who has gradually lost hearing since their fifties may have very different audiological profiles, even if they walk in describing the same symptoms.

Taking that history seriously is what separates a thorough evaluation from a rushed one.

What the evaluation includes

Pure Tone Audiometry

The core of the evaluation. This test measures the softest sounds you can hear across a range of frequencies, from low bass tones to high-pitched sounds. Results are plotted on an audiogram — a graph that maps your hearing across the frequency spectrum and forms the basis of all further recommendations.

Bone Conduction Testing

Identifies where hearing loss originates — in the outer or middle ear, or in the inner ear and hearing nerve. This distinction determines whether hearing loss is conductive, sensorineural, or mixed, and whether medical or surgical referral is appropriate alongside audiological care.

Speech Testing

Goes beyond tones. It measures how clearly you understand spoken words at a comfortable volume and in more demanding listening conditions. Speech-in-noise testing, when indicated, assesses how your auditory system performs in busy restaurants, group conversations, and rooms with background noise.

Middle Ear Testing (Tympanometry)

Evaluates the health and function of the middle ear, including the eardrum and the small bones that transmit sound. It is quick, and it tells us whether fluid, pressure changes, or eardrum abnormalities are contributing to hearing difficulty.

Results Review

Every evaluation concludes with a thorough review of your audiogram and test results, explained in plain terms without clinical shorthand. Dr. Duhon or Dr. Hall walks through what the findings mean for your daily listening environments, discusses recommended next steps, and answers your questions before you leave.

There is no pressure to make decisions at the evaluation. The goal is to make sure you leave with a clear understanding of your hearing health.

Types of hearing loss

Understanding the type of hearing loss identified during your evaluation shapes every recommendation that follows.

Sensorineural

Results from damage to the inner ear or the hearing nerve. It is the most common form of permanent hearing loss in adults and is frequently related to noise exposure, aging, or genetic factors. Hearing aids and audiological management are the primary treatment paths.

Conductive

Occurs when sound is physically blocked or impeded before it reaches the inner ear — earwax buildup, fluid in the middle ear, a perforated eardrum, or structural abnormalities. Many causes are medically treatable, and we coordinate referrals to ENT physicians when appropriate.

Mixed

Involves both sensorineural and conductive components, and requires a treatment plan that addresses both contributing factors.

When to schedule a hearing evaluation

Most people who would benefit from a hearing evaluation are not sure whether their hearing is actually a problem, or they have been adjusting to the gradual change for so long that it feels normal.

A hearing evaluation is appropriate if you:

  • Frequently ask others to repeat themselves, especially in noisy environments
  • Struggle to follow conversations when you cannot see the speaker's face
  • Keep the television at a volume others find too loud
  • Notice that certain voices — the higher-pitched voices of children or women, or speakers on the phone — are harder to understand than others
  • Experience ringing, buzzing, or hissing in your ears
  • Have a history of noise exposure through work, military service, or recreational activities
  • Were told you did not pass a hearing screening

A hearing evaluation is also recommended as a baseline for adults with no current complaints, particularly those with a history of significant noise exposure or a family history of hearing loss.

Hearing evaluations for veterans

Veterans are disproportionately affected by hearing loss and tinnitus, two of the most frequently claimed service-connected disabilities in the VA system. We provide comprehensive hearing evaluations for veterans throughout the Brazos Valley, including Compensation and Pension (C&P) examinations authorized through the VA Community Care Network.

We also extend special discount pricing on hearing technology to active-duty military, veterans, law enforcement officers, firefighters, nurses, EMTs, and all Brazos Valley ISD employees — including teachers, paraprofessionals, bus drivers, cafeteria staff, custodial staff, and all other campus-based employees.

Frequently asked questions

+What is included in a comprehensive hearing evaluation?

A comprehensive hearing evaluation includes a detailed case history, pure tone audiometry across a full range of frequencies, bone conduction testing to identify the origin of hearing loss, speech testing to assess how clearly words are understood, tympanometry to evaluate middle ear health and function, and a thorough review of your results with specific recommendations based on your hearing profile and daily listening needs.

+How long does an adult hearing evaluation take?

Plan for approximately 60 to 90 minutes. We do not schedule evaluations back-to-back, because the time after testing is as important as the testing itself.

+Does a hearing evaluation hurt?

No. A hearing evaluation is entirely non-invasive. Pure tone and speech testing involve listening through headphones and responding to sounds. Tympanometry uses a small probe placed at the entrance of the ear canal that produces a gentle puff of air. No discomfort is involved.

+How should I prepare for my hearing evaluation?

No special preparation is required. It helps to bring a list of current medications, since some drugs affect hearing, and to note the specific situations where hearing difficulty is most noticeable. Bringing a family member or close friend is encouraged — they often provide observations about your hearing that are clinically useful, and they can help you retain the information discussed during the results review.

+What does it mean if my hearing test shows sensorineural hearing loss?

Sensorineural hearing loss indicates damage to the inner ear or hearing nerve and is typically permanent. The most important next step is understanding the degree of loss and how it is affecting daily communication. Depending on the severity and your lifestyle, our recommendations may include hearing aids with real-ear verification fitting, counseling, and ongoing audiological care.

+Will I need a referral to see an audiologist?

Most patients do not need a referral for a hearing evaluation. Patients seeking VA-authorized C&P evaluations will need authorization through the VA Community Care Network. For all other appointments, you can call us directly to schedule at 979-450-7996.

+Does insurance cover hearing evaluations?

Most insurance plans cover medically necessary hearing evaluations and diagnostic testing. We accept Medicare, Blue Cross Blue Shield, United Healthcare, and VA Community Care Network referrals. Our staff verifies your benefits before your appointment and explains any out-of-pocket costs in advance, so there are no surprises.

One appointment can tell you where your hearing stands

Most people spend years adjusting to hearing difficulty before they decide to do something about it. A single appointment can tell you what is contributing to any difficulty you have noticed, and what your options are.

Whether you are coming in for a baseline evaluation, following up on a failed screening, or finally addressing something you have been putting off for years, we are here to help you get connected to the people and moments that matter most.
