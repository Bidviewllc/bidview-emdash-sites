### **Frequently Asked Questions**

#### **Can a medication cause ringing in the ears?**

Yes. Tinnitus is a recognized side effect of several drug classes, including aminoglycoside antibiotics, cisplatin-based chemotherapy, loop diuretics, and high doses of aspirin or ibuprofen.

#### **Will the ringing stop if I stop the medication?**

Sometimes. Ringing tied to high-dose NSAIDs and quinine tends to settle once the dose drops. Damage from aminoglycosides or platinum chemotherapy can be lasting. Never stop a prescription without talking to the prescriber first.

#### **Do blood pressure medications cause tinnitus?**

Tinnitus appears on the side effect listing for some blood pressure medications, and loop diuretics prescribed for heart and kidney conditions are a known ototoxic class. Ask your pharmacist about your specific prescription rather than assuming.

#### **Should I get a hearing test before starting chemotherapy?**

A baseline hearing and balance test before ototoxic treatment gives your team a measurement to compare against during and after treatment, so changes get caught early.

#### **How soon after starting a drug does ringing show up?**

There's no fixed window. Some patients notice it within days, others after weeks of cumulative dosing. Record when the medication started and when the sound started, and bring both dates to your appointment.

#### **Does an audiologist change my medication?**

No. Audiologists test, monitor, and report findings to your physician, oncologist, or pharmacist, who decide whether a prescription should change.
