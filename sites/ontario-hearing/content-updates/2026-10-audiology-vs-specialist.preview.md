## Audiologist vs. Hearing Aid Specialist: What’s the Difference?

An audiologist and a hearing aid specialist both test hearing and fit hearing aids, but they hold different credentials and work under different scopes of practice. An audiologist earns a Doctor of Audiology degree and is licensed to evaluate, diagnose, and treat hearing and balance disorders, including conditions a hearing aid won’t fix.

A hearing aid specialist (also called a hearing instrument specialist) holds a state license to test hearing for the purpose of selecting, fitting, and dispensing hearing aids. The training route and exam for that license are set state by state.

For a patient, the practical question is what you need done. If your hearing has already been evaluated and you want a device fitted, either professional can handle the hearing aid fitting.

If you have dizziness, ringing, drainage, or a sudden change in one ear, start with an audiologist, who can look for the cause and refer you to a physician when one should be involved.

At Ontario Hearing Center, a hearing test is a diagnostic evaluation, not a fitting-only screening.
