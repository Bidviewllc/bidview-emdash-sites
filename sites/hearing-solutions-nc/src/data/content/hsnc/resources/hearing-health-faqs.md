## How do I protect my hearing?

Changes in hearing ability have many different causes and sometimes it cannot always be avoided. However, the good news is that there are fundamental things you can do to ensure that your hearing health is a priority and that precautionary measures are taken.

- Limit your noise exposure and protect your hearing when in loud environments.
- Get annual hearing tests so that you can monitor your hearing health and take action as soon as any changes occur.
- Maintain a healthy lifestyle including exercise and proper diet.
- Avoid nicotine use.

## Do changes in hearing ability only affect older people?

People of all ages can experience changes in hearing ability, however the average age is over 65 years old. There are 6 million individuals with hearing difficulties in the US between the ages of 18-44. The World Health Organization has reported the rise of cases among younger people, in no small part due to the widespread use of smartphones and earbuds!

## Would my primary care physician check my hearing?

Only 13% of doctors routinely check their patient's hearing. Many people find it more difficult to hear in noisy situations. Therefore, in quiet environments like a doctor's office, most people can hear just fine, so it can be challenging for your PCP to recognize hearing difficulties. As such, it is important to include a [hearing evaluation](/services#hearing-loss) in your annual healthcare checkups.

## Does hearing loss cause tinnitus?

While tinnitus and decreases in hearing ability are often experienced simultaneously, one does not cause the other. Research suggests that both tinnitus and changes in hearing share the same root causes (often the damage of inner ear cells), so it's not surprising they often show up together. Tinnitus and hearing difficulties are both treatable - contact us to learn more!

## Can hearing be improved?

This question never fails to make it to our hearing health FAQs list. It really depends on the cause of the hearing difficulty. In some instances, hearing challenges caused by blockages or impacted earwax can be “cured” once the blockages are removed. Sometimes surgery can be done to correct medical problems in the outer or middle ear.

However, sensorineural hearing loss is by far more common. There are many causes including medical conditions, age-related and noise-induced hearing loss to name a few. The delicate hair cells in the inner ear are unable to repair themselves which results in certain sounds and frequencies no longer within your hearing range. The most common solution is to amplify those sounds putting them back into your hearing range.

## Can hearing devices completely fix my hearing?

" I need hearing aids. Now what?"

If you have been diagnosed with hearing loss, one of the first recommendations would be to get a hearing aid.

[Hearing aids](/patient-resources/hearing-aid-overview) help you interact with the world around you, but they do not "fix" your hearing, just as your nearsightedness or farsightedness is not fixed when wearing glasses.

Think of hearing devices as a solution that supports all aspects of your life!

## Do I need two hearing aids?

This is another popular hearing aids FAQ. Some of our patients ask, "Do hearing aids come in pairs?"

Before getting to the answer, you need to understand that human hearing is binaural. This means that we pick up sounds with both of our ears. While unilateral issues do exist (in one ear), binaural issues are far more common. Binaural hearing loss is treated with the use of two hearing aids. When it comes to hearing, it is important to have a comprehensive hearing test that accurately determines your hearing abilities. We’re here to help you find the best solution for your needs.

While some cheap hearing aids may be worth a try for certain individuals, others may require more advanced or powerful hearing aids to address their specific hearing loss. ## Can you self-refer to an audiologist?

In most cases, you can self-refer to an audiologist. This simply means that you can make an appointment with an audiologist without a referral from a healthcare provider. However, it's important to check with your insurance provider to see if they require a referral before they will cover the cost of your visit. Some insurance plans may also have specific audiologists accredited, so it's a good idea to check with your insurance provider to find out which audiologists are covered under your plan.

## How can I tell if my hearing is bad?

Have you been noticing some red flags in your hearing health lately? Below are several signs that may indicate that you have hearing loss:

- Difficulty hearing conversations, especially in noisy environments.
- Turning up the volume on the radio or TV louder than others in the same room would prefer.
- Struggling to understand soft or low-volume speech.
- Finding it challenging to hear high-pitched sounds like a child/woman’s voice.

If you are experiencing any of these symptoms, you might want to consider getting your hearing tested by an audiologist. Audiologists can help determine if you have hearing loss and recommend appropriate treatments, such as hearing aids, cochlear implants, or assistive listening devices to help improve your hearing.

## What is the best hearing aid?

Hearing aids come in many different styles, brands, and technology. Since hearing loss is unique for each individual, it’s impossible to name “the best” hearing aid. Realistically speaking, the best hearing aid will depend on an individual’s unique type and degree of hearing loss, budget, personal preferences, and lifestyle.
