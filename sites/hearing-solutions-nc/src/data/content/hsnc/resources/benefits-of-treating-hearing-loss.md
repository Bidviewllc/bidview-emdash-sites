## Improved Interpersonal Relationships

When speech sounds are no longer in your hearing range, it affects your speech recognition abilities and thus, how you communicate with others.

It can become difficult to follow what people say and differentiate between similar sounds.

Group discussions can be very difficult, especially in busy, noisy environments.

The good news is that treating hearing loss benefits your most important relationships.

[Hearing aids](/patient-resources/hearing-aid-overview) have outstanding speech recognition capabilities, which help whether you are engaging in serious discussions or listening to your grandkid’s funny story on a video chat.

When you being to appreciate the benefits of treating hearing loss, you will realize that it is a commitment to your loved ones as it is to your happiness and wellbeing.

## Improved Cognitive Health

Many studies have shown a link between untreated hearing loss and a greater risk of developing dementia.

In part, as mentioned above, hearing challenges can interfere with your social life - and we certainly don't want to be isolated from our favorite people and activities!

Our brain and our hearing are intricately connected. One of the many benefits of treating hearing loss includes your brain being saved from listening fatigue.

With the right hearing treatment, you can focus on the things you love and care about!

Researchers have found encouraging evidence amongst their test subjects: people who address their hearing issues with the use of hearing aids are at a lower risk of developing dementia.

## Improved Personal Safety

Your sense of hearing is also connected to your vestibular system, which manages your balance.

Additionally, your brain relies on environmental sounds to shape your sense of space.

[Research](https://leader.pubs.asha.org/do/10.1044/hearing-loss-and-the-increased-risk-of-falls/full/#:~:text=Hearing%20loss%20is%20one%20of,10%20decibels%20of%20hearing%20loss.) shows that you are more vulnerable to falls and accidents when you have untreated hearing loss.

The good news is that this risk is considerably decreased when you use hearing aids!

A [report](https://source.wustl.edu/2014/12/hearing-aids-may-improve-balance/) by the Washington University School of Medicine in St. Louis reveals that wearing hearing aids decreases the likelihood of falling, which contributes to your overall personal safety.

## Improved Physical Independence

If you consider how you use our sense of hearing in daily activities and interactions, you can understand how much you depend on it.

You wake up to the sound of an alarm clock. The whistle of a tea kettle notifies you that the water is boiling. A beep on the stove tells you the cookies are ready to come out of the oven.

Indeed, even crossing the street involves spatial awareness.

Treating hearing loss affords you a better awareness of your environment, and it improves your sense of confidence and freedom in public places.

Treating hearing loss through hearing aids also keeps you more active.

When you’re connected with your surroundings, you’re more likely to pursue the activities you love.

## Improved Earning Power

Approximately 60% of the American workforce experiences some degree of [hearing loss](/patient-resources/hearing-loss). Untreated hearing loss can affect your ability to communicate with your colleagues, your productivity on the job, and your ability to work in a team.

Recently, studies have indicated that [treating hearing loss](/blog) helps maintain one’s earning power. In one report, a comparative analysis of salary levels between people with hearing difficulties revealed that people who used hearing aids earned $5,000-$6,000 more annually than peers who did not treat their hearing.

In other words, treating hearing loss is worth the investment!
