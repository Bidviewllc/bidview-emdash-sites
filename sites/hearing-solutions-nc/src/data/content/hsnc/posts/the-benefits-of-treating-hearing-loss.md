If you have found that you’ve had to ask people to repeat themselves more often than not, there is a good chance that you are dealing with hearing loss. It is common for people to avoid treatment or deny that they have hearing loss altogether. However, the benefits of treating hearing loss greatly outweigh any reason to avoid dealing with this condition.

The most common treatment for hearing loss is hearing aids, which amplify the sounds around you so you can communicate clearly again. However, it generally takes people seven to ten years for people to deal with hearing loss once they suspect it’s present. Even more alarming, is that of those who could benefit from treatment of their hearing loss with hearing aids, only 20% wear them. Here are just a few of the key benefits of treating your hearing loss sooner rather than waiting.

### Improve Your Relationships

While hearing loss is an issue that begins in the ears it ultimately affects communication. This means that as hearing loss becomes more severe all the relationships in your life are at risk for being affected. This includes your relationships with friends, family, co-workers and even your significant other. Treating hearing loss can help enhance and restore your communication skills which mean that you can reconnect and build better relationships.

### Improved Intimacy

It may not seem like a problem at first, but miscommunications can cause tension in relationships. Hearing loss can cause a cascade of detrimental effects and negative emotions between partners such as loneliness, resentment and contempt. Not only this, but unbeknownst to many, it is the small talk and inside jokes which builds a sense of closeness, belonging and intimacy.

When this erodes due to untreated hearing loss, it can cause long term partners and lovers to feel distant from one another. The good news is that treating hearing loss with hearing aids will allow you to hear your loved ones again. You can start to rebuild closeness overtime and heal rifts which may have formed. The sooner you start this process the better.

### Improved Sense of Belonging

Relationships can also erode amongst family and friends. As hearing loss develops your brain is forced to work harder to put together words with limited information. This is not only frustrating but incredibly exhausting. It is easier for many who live with undiagnosed or untreated hearing loss to avoid social situations altogether rather than subjecting themselves to the struggle of hearing.

This means that you may start to miss family gatherings, celebrations and friendships may feel strained or distant. You may also avoid joining clubs and engaging in hobbies which are social in nature due to your hearing condition. Treating hearing loss will enable you to regain social stamina and confidence. You will be able to hear and respond easily to your family and friends and begin to feel more connected.

### Improved Sense of Independence

It is all too common for those with untreated hearing loss to rely on others as sort of a translator when communicating out in the world. This means that it can be challenging to head out on your own. It can also be difficult to navigate the world on your own and actually a safety concern.

Treating hearing loss with hearing aids not only helps you communicate, but helps you understand where sounds are coming from and how close they are. This is called sound localization and can become compromised, especially as hearing loss becomes worse in one ear and not the other. When people’s sense of space is distorted, it can lead to higher risk of accidents, falls and hospitalizations. Driving a car becomes a major safety concern. However, treating hearing loss can even out unilateral hearing loss and help improve your awareness in the world.

### Support Your Brain Health

The strain on hearing has been found to cause cognitive decline. In fact, studies show that even a mild case of hearing loss can cause people to score lower on cognitive health testing. This is connected to the amount of energy the brain is forced to spend interpreting sound with limited information. This causes exhaustion and for the brain to draw resources from other areas. However, treating hearing loss through hearing aids or cochlear implants can help you struggle less and hear clearer, reducing the extra strain on your brain and keeping you sharp for years to come.
