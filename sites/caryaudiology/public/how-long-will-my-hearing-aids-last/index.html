How long will my hearing aids last?

  • June 5, 2024

If you’re someone who has recently invested in hearing aids, you may be wondering how long you can expect them to last. The lifespan of hearing aids can vary depending on a number of factors, including the type of hearing aid, the manufacturer, and how well they are maintained. In this blog, we will explore the different types of hearing aids, what factors can impact their longevity, and tips for maintaining your hearing aids to ensure they last as long as possible. 

We’ll also explore some options for hearing aid repair and replacement. Whether you’re a new hearing aid user or have been wearing them for some time, this blog will provide valuable information on how to get the most out of your hearing aids.

Different Types of Hearing Aids

To better understand how hearing aids work and how long they can last, you need to be familiar with the different types of hearing aids available on the market. Each of these hearing aids has its own unique features and benefits. 

Some of the most common types of hearing aids include:

  • Behind-the-ear (BTE) hearing aids: These are the most common type of hearing aid and sit behind the ear. 
  • In-the-ear (ITE) hearing aids: These hearing aids are custom-made to fit inside the ear canal. 
  • In-the-canal (ITC) hearing aids: These hearing aids are custom-made to fit partly in the ear canal. 
  • Completely-in-canal (CIC) hearing aids: These hearing aids are custom-made to fit entirely in the ear canal. 

Receiver-in-canal (RIC) hearing aids: This type of hearing aid has a small wire that goes into the ear canal and the rest of the device sits behind the ear. 

It is worth noting that the longevity of hearing aids also depends on the quality of the device and the manufacturer. Premium hearing aids typically have a longer lifespan than entry-level models.

Additionally, proper maintenance, such as regular cleaning and battery replacement, can also extend the lifespan of hearing aids.

How do I know when my hearing aids need replacing?

There are several signs that may indicate that your hearing aids need to be replaced, including:

  • Reduced sound quality or volume: If you notice that your hearing aids are not producing sound as clearly or as loudly as they used to, it may be time to replace them.
  • Frequent repairs: If you find yourself having to repair your hearing aids frequently, it may be more cost-effective to replace them instead.
  • Battery life: If your hearing aids are not lasting as long on a single charge or battery replacement as they used to, it could be a sign that the battery is no longer holding a charge and should be replaced.
  • Physical wear and tear: If your hearing aids are showing signs of physical wear and tear, such as cracks or damage to the casing, it may be time to replace them.

It’s also a good idea to have your hearing aids serviced and checked by a professional every year to ensure they are working properly.

Do hearing aids become less effective over time?

Hearing aids can become less effective over time for a variety of reasons. Here are a few examples:

  • Battery life: Hearing aids rely on batteries to function, and as the battery drains, the hearing aid may become less effective.
  • Ear wax buildup: Ear wax can accumulate on the hearing aid and in the ear canal, which can cause the hearing aid to produce muffled or distorted sounds. Cary Audiology provides professional ear wax removal services in Cary, NC.
  • Damage: Hearing aids can be damaged by exposure to moisture, heat, or physical impact. Damage to the hearing aid can cause it to produce poor sound quality or stop working altogether.
  • Accumulation of dirt and dust: Hearing aids are exposed to the environment, and over time, dirt and dust can accumulate in the device, which can cause it to produce poor sound quality or stop working altogether.
  • Wear and tear: With regular use, the components of a hearing aid can wear out over time, which can cause it to produce poor sound quality or stop working altogether.

It’s also important to note that as your hearing changes over time, your hearing aids may not be as effective as they were before, which is why it’s important to have your hearing tested regularly and update your hearing aids accordingly.
